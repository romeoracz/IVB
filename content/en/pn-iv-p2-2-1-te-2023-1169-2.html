Genetically engineered Natural Killer cells - a new tool for cancer immunotherapy (NKImmunoTher)

Abstract

Scientific context: Metastasis is the main cause of death in cancer and sustained effort is needed to manage this disease. The innate ability of NK cells to eliminate tumour cells has made them the best candidate for cancer immunotherapy, with NK cell-based immunotherapies having been researched for years. The development and progression of cancer is influenced by the relationship between the tumour and the host immune system, with a number of mechanisms contributing to the suppression of cellular immunity. One of the most important mechanisms is the production of inhibitory cytokines, such as TGF-β. This cytokine can strongly affect NK cell activity, reducing cytotoxic capacity and impairing antitumour activity. Blocking or loss of the type 2 TGF-β receptor on NK cells can restore cytotoxic function.

Aim: This project aims to develop cytokine-activated TGFR2 knockout NK cells with improved antitumour activity for controlling cancer progression and metastasis.

NK cells will be genetically modified using CRISPR/Cas9 technology to eliminate the TGFR2 genes, which will confer resistance to the inhibitory effect exerted by TGF-β. The TGFR2 knockout NK cells will be activated with a cytokine complex to acquire durability and enhanced effector functions. The genetically modified NK cells will be validated in murine melanoma models.

OBJECTIVES and RESULTS

Objectives

O1. Generation of TGFBR2 knockout NK cells to be activated with cytokines

O2. In vitro validation of the improved antitumour activity of cytokine-activated TGFBR2 knockout NK cells

O3. Validation of cytokine-activated TGFBR2 knockout NK cells as a potential immunotherapeutic candidate for cancer therapy using mouse melanoma models

Project implementation scheme

Expected results:

  • Generation of NK cells with improved antitumour activity
  • NK cells validated for cancer therapy
  • New NK cell-based immunotherapy for melanoma therapy
