Abstract

Molecular tests are essential for a complete diagnosis and targeted treatment of the patient. The body’s response to treatment is difficult to anticipate; therefore, patients must be monitored through molecular tests to determine whether the treatment is effective or needs to be changed. The large-scale application of modern sequencing methods has led to major advances in molecular diagnosis. Thus, new mutations associated with different types of oncological pathologies have been discovered, allowing better diagnosis and more effective prognosis. The field is constantly evolving, and established sets of mutations are reclassified annually with regard to their use in diagnosis and prognosis. The general aim of the project is to produce molecular kits suited to modern PCR methods for the identification of cellular genetic fingerprints. The study is designed so that (1) the results obtained are useful in laboratory medicine, (2) the genes tested are characteristic of a diverse spectrum of tumours, (3) and the optimised technique is applicable to the quantification of several types of genetic mutations.

Objectives

The general objective of the project is to produce and optimise kits for the assessment of genetic fingerprints (mutations) by modern PCR techniques, namely TaqMan, ddPCR and rhPCR. Specific objectives: establishing methods for analysing genetic anomalies for which no such procedures exist in the clinical laboratory; new methods with improved performance compared with those currently used in the clinic.
