Requests for information of public interest under Law no. 544/2001 on free access to information of public interest

Free and unrestricted access by any person to any information of public interest, as defined by Law no. 544/2001, is one of the fundamental principles of the relationship between individuals and public authorities, in accordance with the Constitution of Romania and with the international documents ratified by the Parliament of Romania.

Law no. 544/2001 is applied in line with the following principles

  1. Transparency – public authorities and institutions must carry out their activity openly towards the public, so that free and unrestricted access to information of public interest is the rule and limiting access to information is the exception, under the conditions of the law;
  2. Uniform application – public authorities and institutions ensure that the law is applied uniformly, in accordance with its provisions and with these methodological norms;
  3. Autonomy – each public authority or institution draws up its own rules of organisation and operation for its information and public relations departments, in accordance with the law and with these methodological norms.
    Information of public interest means any information that concerns or results from the activities of a public authority or public institution, whatever the medium, form or way in which the information is expressed.

The request form or administrative complaint will be forwarded for resolution to the person designated with responsibilities under Law 544/2001, on the basis of the relevant legal provisions.

How to appeal under Law no. 544/2001 on free access to information of public interest

If a person considers that their right of access to information of public interest has been infringed, they may submit an administrative complaint (model 1 or model 2) to the head of the authority from which the information was requested, in accordance with art. 21 para. (2) of Law no. 544/2001 on free access to information of public interest, as subsequently amended and supplemented, and with art. 32-34 of Government Decision no. 123/2002 approving the Methodological norms for the application of Law no. 544/2001 on free access to information of public interest. Model administrative complaints are found in annexes 2a) and 2b) of the Methodological norms.

Ways of challenging the authority’s decision when a person considers that their right of access to the requested information of public interest has been harmed.

Legal basis: art. 21 of Law no. 544 of 12.10.2001.

  1. An explicit or tacit refusal by the employee designated by a public authority or institution to apply the provisions of this law is a disciplinary offence and makes the person at fault liable to disciplinary action.
  2. A complaint against the refusal referred to in para. (1) may be submitted to the head of the public authority or institution concerned within 30 days of the injured person becoming aware of it.
  3. If, after the administrative investigation, the complaint proves well founded, the reply is sent to the injured person within 15 days of the complaint being submitted and will contain both the information of public interest originally requested and a mention of the disciplinary sanctions taken against the person at fault.
    In addition, if the applicant considers that their rights under the law have been harmed, they may file a complaint with the administrative litigation section of the tribunal in whose area they live or in whose area the institution or public authority has its seat, as provided by art. 22 para. (1) of Law no. 544/2001, as subsequently amended and supplemented, and art. 36 of Government Decision no. 123/2002.

Legal basis: art. 22 of Law no. 544 of 12.10.2001.

  1. A person who considers that their rights under this law have been harmed may file a complaint with the administrative litigation section of the tribunal in whose area they live or in whose area the public authority or institution has its seat. The complaint must be filed within 30 days of the expiry of the period set out in art. 7.
  2. The court may order the public authority or institution to provide the requested information of public interest and to pay moral and/or material damages.
  3. The tribunal’s judgment may be appealed.
  4. The decision of the Court of Appeal is final and irrevocable.
  5. Both the complaint and the appeal are heard by the court under an urgent procedure and are exempt from stamp duty.
