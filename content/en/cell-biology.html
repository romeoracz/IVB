The effect of gut microbiota products and functional amyloid proteins on pathological protein aggregation and on prion-like propagation of pathology in models of neurodegeneration

Contract no. 1N/2019

Funding: Ministry of Education and Research

Duration: 2019-2022

Within the “Core” Programme: Systemic approaches to the identification and/or validation of molecular targets for therapeutic strategies in pathologies with a major impact on health – SISPATH;

Objective 2. Experimental and functional models for systemic approaches in pathologies with major health implications

Project Leader: Prof. Dr. Bogdan O. Popescu

Brief presentation of the project

Neurodegenerative diseases are currently a major health problem and a priority research topic worldwide. Neurodegenerative diseases occur frequently in the adult population over 65 years of age, are responsible for progressive irreversible disability and represent a significant socio-economic burden. To date there is no approved therapeutic intervention, or one with certain positive results, that stops or slows the progression of these diseases, only symptomatic therapies, with clear but often modest benefits. The results of recent clinical and experimental studies have shown the existence of possible causal links between various particularities of gut microbiota composition and neurodegenerative diseases, especially Parkinson’s disease. The substrate of these causal links is mediated both by certain microbiota products (for example, functional amyloid proteins, biosurfactants, endotoxins) and by endogenous proteins whose expression can be modulated/induced by the microbiota (for example, calprotectin is an inflammatory marker, but also a potentially amyloidogenic protein). Also, the dysfunction that occurs between the host and the gut microbiota (dysbiosis with an intestinal inflammatory reaction) could produce neuronal changes favourable to neurodegeneration (for example, changes secondary to the alteration of the intestinal barrier and/or the blood-brain barrier, or as a result of disruption of the microbiota-gut-brain axis, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, etc.). None of these aspects has so far been investigated in detail, and they represent a research topic of interest.

Aim of the project:

 To characterise the effect of functional bacterial amyloid proteins and other amyloidogenic products of the gut microbiota on pathological protein aggregation and on prion-like propagation of pathology in models of neurodegeneration, with a view to identifying new biomarkers and molecular targets and developing prophylactic strategies and personalised therapies in sporadic Parkinson’s disease and other neurodegenerative diseases, thus contributing to an improved approach to pathologies with a major impact on health.

Objectives of the project:

  1. Development and improvement of the protocol for a standardised testing/immunoassay of the bacterial amyloidogenic products of interest.
  2. Assessment of the association between the faecal level of the bacterial amyloidogenic products of interest, the level of faecal calprotectin and Parkinson’s disease in the early motor phase – an observational analytical case-control study.
  3. Confirmation of the results on the association between the faecal level of bacterial amyloidogenic products, the calprotectin level and Parkinson’s disease, and testing of possible associations with other neurodegenerative proteinopathies.
  4. Characterisation of the effects of gut microbiota amyloidogenic products associated with early motor phase Parkinson’s disease and of calprotectin on alpha-synuclein aggregation and internalisation/propagation, in vitro, in cell lines, in the presence and absence of rotenone .
  5. Assessment of the effect of gut microbiota amyloidogenic products associated with Parkinson’s disease, calprotectin and short-chain fatty acids on clinical manifestations, intestinal pathology and brain pathology in vivo, in murine models of Parkinson’s disease, under wild gut microbiota conditions, using protocols based on the results of the in vitro studies.
  6. Assessment of the effect of gut microbiota amyloidogenic products associated with Parkinson’s disease, calprotectin and short-chain fatty acids on clinical manifestations, intestinal pathology and brain pathology in murine models of Parkinson’s disease, under controlled gut microbiota conditions, using protocols based on the previous results.
  7. Assessment of the effect of gut microbiota amyloidogenic products (selected according to the previous results), calprotectin and short-chain fatty acids on cell lines and murine models of other neurodegenerative diseases involving the misfolding and pathological aggregation of certain proteins.
  8. Development of an etiopathogenic model of the involvement of bacterial amyloidogenic proteins, other amyloidogenic products of the gut microbiota and calprotectin in Parkinson’s disease, with extrapolation to Alzheimer’s disease and other neurodegenerative proteinopathies.

Expected results:

  1. faecal biomarkers for the early motor stage of Parkinson’s disease – these may be microbiota biomarkers or mixed host-microbiota biomarkers.
  2. faecal biomarkers for the early motor stage of Parkinson’s disease confirmed in another group of patients, with a known specificity relative to other neurodegenerative diseases.
  3. an optimised experimental paradigm for assessing the effects of gut microbiota products and other molecules on alpha-synuclein aggregation, internalisation and propagation under different experimental conditions.
  4. an intermediate etiopathogenic model for Parkinson’s/Alzheimer’s disease regarding the causal relationship between exposure to bacterial amyloidogenic products, pathological aggregation of alpha-synuclein and the prion-like spread of pathology from the gut to the brain.
  5. an optimised murine model for the study of the gut microbiota in Parkinson’s disease.
  6. an etiopathogenic model of the involvement of functional bacterial amyloid proteins and other amyloidogenic products of the gut microbiota in pathological protein aggregation and in the prion-like propagation of pathology in Parkinson’s disease, extrapolated to Alzheimer’s disease and other neurodegenerative diseases involving abnormal proteins.
  7. a list of new potential molecular targets for personalised prophylactic therapeutic interventions/strategies, or for stopping/slowing the progression of neurodegenerative diseases, identified within the project.
