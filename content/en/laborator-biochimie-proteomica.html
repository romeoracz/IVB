Biochemistry - Proteomics Laboratory

The main research theme of the Biochemistry-Proteomics Laboratory focuses on molecular mechanisms in solid cancers (mainly highly aggressive tumours, such as pancreatic adenocarcinoma and glioblastoma), particularly on cell signalling. Our main point of interest is the identification of signalling nodes with an impact on tumour cell proliferation that can be modulated by inhibitors. To this end, we focus on the multiplex detection of intracellular proteins relevant to oncogenic cell signalling, as well as on the assessment, as a secondary mechanism, of chronic inflammation.

Secondly, we have established various collaborations with SMEs, as part of implementing expertise in biomedical research, technology transfer and innovation towards the private sector in the fields of health and medical biotechnology. As a result of these collaborations, we are now specialised in performing cytotoxicity assays of various purified compounds and assessing their proliferative effect.

As a future research direction, we aim to develop tumour spheroids and organoids, including gene-edited ones, as tools for a better understanding of the roles in oncogenesis of genes that are not traditionally known as proto-oncogenes. By combining the two methods, we will be able to obtain more precise information on cell proliferation, growth rates and the invasiveness of malignant cells.
