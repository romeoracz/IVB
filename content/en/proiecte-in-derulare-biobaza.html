PN 19.29.02.03

Molecular signature of the anti-tumour response in experimental models of metastasis

Within the proposed topic, we will evaluate in vitro and in vivo the direct influence of CD36+ tumour cells on the cytotoxic function of NK cells. Also, taking into account the predilection of different types of cancer for a particular metastatic site, we will investigate, in an experimental model of metastasis, whether CD36+ tumour cells show a predisposition to colonise a particular organ and, if so, which factors determine this behaviour. For this we will use a series of wild-type and genetically modified mouse strains, under standard diet conditions or with a modified diet. Finally, we will test in a murine model of metastasis with human tumour cells whether the data obtained in the murine model can be extrapolated to humans.

Aim of the project: the preclinical study of the molecular profile of NK cells for monitoring the antimetastatic response in solid tumours and the identification of possible markers for targeted antitumour therapy.

Main objectives of the project:

  • identification of possible molecular markers on NK cells for monitoring the antimetastatic response;
  • identification of factors that may influence organ-specific metastasis;
  • new evidence regarding the biomarker role of CD36 in the metastatic process.
