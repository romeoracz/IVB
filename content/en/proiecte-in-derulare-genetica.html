COFUND-ERANET E-RARE 3-HETER-OMICS-2, 88/2019

Multi-OMICS approach to cerebral cortical malformations

Total value of the contract: 585.063,00 lei, representing 125.550,00 euro

Implementation period: 01/06/2019-31/05/2022

Team: Aurora Arghir, PhD; Sorina Mihaela Papuc, PhD; Maria Dobre, PhD; Alina Erbescu

Abstract

Heterotopia is an abnormal positioning of neurons in the cerebral cortex. Heterotopias are rare neurodevelopmental diseases that lead to severe forms of epilepsy and intellectual disability. These disorders form a spectrum ranging from abnormal neuronal clusters at the level of the cerebral ventricles (periventricular) to heterotopias located in the white matter (subcortical). In order to investigate the specific, convergent pathways underlying these disorders, it is very important to understand the cell types that function abnormally and their abnormal developmental trajectories, as well as to characterise their functional consequences. In addition, the accumulation of clinical and molecular data from previously uninvestigated patients offers an important clinical perspective. The aim of this project is to genetically characterise a cohort of children with heterotopia and to correlate the genetic data with the clinical data. Through multi-dimensional data integration, this project will allow the identification of new variants / candidate genes and potential therapeutic sources. In addition, the families of these patients will benefit from the possibility of a better clinical and genetic characterisation.

Objective

Comprehensive genetic characterisation of a new cohort of patients with heterotopias and correlation with clinical and imaging data

Stage 1 Dec 2019

Malformations of cerebral cortical development (MDC) are an important cause of epilepsy and developmental delay. Among MCDs, heterotopias are genetically heterogeneous conditions, with a wide range of reported defects, from microdeletions and microduplications to point mutations, which requires the use of a panel of genetic investigation techniques able to detect both quantitative genomic anomalies and sequence anomalies.

Objectives of stage 1

– Initiation of the study group and of the collection of primary and processed biological samples obtained from patients with heterotopia

– Optimisation of the experimental protocols for targeted mutational screening by classical sequencing

– Establishment of the protocol for genomic investigation by chromosomal microarray

Methodologies used and results obtained

In the current stage, activities were carried out to initiate the database and the collection of biological samples, as well as to set up and optimise molecular tests based on sequencing and microarray. Patients with different forms of heterotopia were included in the study, with biological samples available from the probands and their parents. Preliminary processing of the biological samples and DNA isolation were carried out. The direct sequencing method for sequence analysis of the FLNA and DCX genes was optimised using the Applied Biosystems ABI 3500 Genetic Analyzer capillary electrophoresis system, and the working protocol for the subsequent stages was established. Genomic screening for DNA copy number anomalies was initiated by microarray-based comparative genomic hybridisation (array-CGH), performed on the Agilent SureScan platform. The first genomic profiles were obtained with high-density oligonucleotide microarray slides, OGT 4x180K, and the genomic imbalances were analysed.

Conclusions

In this stage, the proposed stage objectives were fully achieved.

– The database of patients with heterotopias was initiated and data from the patients included up to the reporting date were recorded.

– Uniform procedures for processing and DNA extraction from the collected biological samples were established and the project's nucleic acid collection was initiated.

– Activities were carried out to optimise the Sanger sequencing technique for the FLNA and DCX genes using control DNA without changes in the tested genes, and the working protocol was established.

– The working protocol was established and array-CGH-based genomic screening was initiated.

– Scientific communications were presented in the form of two posters and one oral presentation at various scientific events in the fields of genetics and neuropsychiatry (the XIth Conference of Medical Genetics with International Participation, Timisoara, and the XXVIIth National Conference of the Romanian Association for the Fight Against Epilepsy, Bucharest).

Stage 2 Dec 2020

Malformations of cerebral cortical development (MDC) are an important cause of epilepsy and developmental delay. Among MCDs, heterotopias are genetically heterogeneous conditions, with a wide range of reported defects, from (micro)deletions and (micro)duplications to sequence anomalies, which requires the use of a panel of genetic investigation techniques able to detect both genomic imbalances and sequence variations.

Objectives of stage 2

– Completion of the collection of biological samples

– Mutational screening by classical sequencing of the FLNA gene

– Genomic screening for DNA copy number anomalies, based on array CGH

– Optimisation of the experimental protocols for mutational screening by next-generation whole-exome sequencing

– Dissemination of the results obtained

Methodologies used and results obtained

In the current stage, activities were carried out for the genetic characterisation of the patients included in the study and for the optimisation of molecular tests based on next-generation whole-exome sequencing.

Activities to complete the database and the collection of biological samples were continued by receiving and processing biological samples from 10 patients with heterotopias and their parents.

Chromosomal microarray experiments (4x180k slides, OGT Constitutional v3) were performed for 12 patients, and one pathogenic CNV and three CNVs of uncertain significance were identified. Mutational screening of the FLNA gene was performed by classical Sanger sequencing in 3 patients, without detecting pathogenic sequence variants or variants of uncertain significance in the amplicons analysed.

The working protocol for whole-exome sequencing was adapted and optimised, using a PCR-based library preparation method with the AmpliSeq for Illumina Exome Panel kit. This method allows high, uniform and specific coverage (>90%) to be obtained, with a large volume of data (approx. 59 MB). Library preparation for sequencing was carried out for 8 biological samples from 3 patients and their parents.

Dissemination activities were carried out by participating in the European Human Genetics Conference with a poster ( „ Brain malformations diagnosis and clinical management in pediatric patients”) and by submitting for publication to the journal Experimental and Therapeutical Medicine a manuscript, “Clinical and genomic findings in brain heterotopia – report of a pediatric patient cohort from Romania”, currently under review, with a decision of minor revision.

In addition, on 28-29 January 2020 the working meeting of the HETEROMICS consortium, coordinated by Prof Fiona Francis, took place in Paris. The institutions and research groups involved in the consortium presented materials describing the team and the research topics addressed in general, as well as describing the activities carried out in the first stage of the project and the degree of achievement of the phase objectives.

Conclusions

– In this stage, the proposed stage objectives were fully achieved.

– The study group and the collection of biological samples were completed;

– Mutational screening by classical sequencing of the FLNA gene was performed in patients with suggestive clinical and imaging data, and data analysis and interpretation of the significance of the detected sequence variants were carried out;

– Genomic screening by array CGH was performed for all patients in study group 1 and additionally for 4 patients in group 2. The genomic profiles were analysed and the CNVs were classified on the basis of clinical significance;

– The experimental protocol for next-generation whole-exome sequencing was optimised and 8 biological samples were sequenced;

– The results of the activities carried out within the project were disseminated at scientific events and submitted for publication to specialist journals.

Stage 3 Dec 2021

Congenital malformations, developmental delay and intellectual disability are the most frequent medical problems in the paediatric population and represent a heterogeneous group of diseases with a significant impact on quality of life. Malformations of cortical development (MCD) frequently have a genetic cause, with the progress of investigation technologies constantly bringing new information on the genetic architecture of these conditions. The characterisation of genetic defects in MCD contributes to deciphering the pathogenetic pathways and has the potential to identify molecular targets for personalised therapies. At the same time, elucidating the genetic aetiology can contribute to optimising clinical and therapeutic management and to assessing the prognosis and the course of the disease.

Objectives of stage 3

  • Expansion of the collection of biological samples;
  • Mutational screening by classical sequencing of the FLNA and DCX genes in patients with a clinical and imaging profile suggestive of defects in these genes;
  • Genomic screening for DNA copy number anomalies, based on array CGH;
  • Mutational screening by next-generation whole-exome sequencing (Whole Exome Sequencing – WES) in patients selected on the basis of the clinical and imaging phenotype, without other genetic changes relevant to the brain malformation;
  • Analysis and interpretation of the data obtained;
  • Dissemination of the results obtained.

Methodologies used and results obtained

In the current stage, activities were carried out for the genetic characterisation of the patients included in the study, including investigations based on next-generation whole-exome sequencing in selected patients. Activities to complete the database and the collection of biological samples were continued. In this stage, biological samples were received from 10 patients with heterotopias and their parents.

The genomic profile was characterised by chromosomal microarray studies for 12 biological samples, using 4x180K microarray slides (OGT Constitutional v3, with a design specific to genetic developmental diseases). Genomic screening by array CGH was completed for all patients, with the identification, in this stage, of some variants of uncertain significance.

All patients with phenotypes suggestive for targeted genetic testing were investigated by classical Sanger sequencing: three patients using primers for the coding regions of the FLNA gene and two patients using primers for the exons of the DCX gene. No pathogenic sequence variants or variants of uncertain significance were detected in the FLNA gene. A pathogenic variant was detected in the gene DCX, in association with subcortical band heterotopia, in a female patient with a phenotype characteristic of impaired function of the gene DCX.

The library preparation protocol for sequencing was carried out for 8 selected biological samples. The libraries obtained were denatured and sequenced on the NextSeq 550 platform (Ilumina) with the NextSeq 500/550 300-cycle kit (access and logistic support provided by members of the CRGM Dolj team of SCJU Craiova and UMF Craiova). The data were analysed and interpreted using dedicated software, in successive steps on two modules, individual analysis (single case) and comparative analysis (trio analysis). Particular attention was paid to genes known to be involved in the occurrence of brain malformations, followed by the step of querying the whole exome for potential candidate genes.

Dissemination activities were carried out by presenting one paper each at the European Cytogenomics Conference, the Excellence in Pediatrics Conference, the European Congress of Psychiatry, the National Congress of Paediatrics and the Conference of the Romanian Society Against Epilepsy. An original scientific article entitled “Clinical and genomic findings in brain heterotopia – report of a pediatric patient cohort from Romania” was published in the journal Experimental and Therapeutic Medicine (1 December 2021, https://doi.org/10.3892/etm.2021.11024). In addition, a manuscript with original results entitled „Novel DCX pathogenic variant in a girl with subcortical band heterotopia” was submitted for review to the journal Revista Romana de Medicina de Laborator, indexed in Web of Science (impact factor 1.027). In the first semester of 2022, the article was accepted for publication.

On 13-14 January 2021, the virtual working meeting of the HETEROMICS consortium, coordinated by Prof Fiona Francis, Sorbonne University, Paris, took place. The institutions and research groups involved in the consortium presented the intermediate results obtained in the second stage of the project and the degree of achievement of the phase objectives.

In conclusion, in the second stage all the proposed stage objectives were achieved:

  • The collection of biological samples was completed;
  • Mutational screening by classical sequencing of the FLNA and DCX genes was performed in all patients with clinical and imaging data suggestive for targeted investigation of these genes, and data analysis and interpretation of the significance of the detected sequence variants were carried out;
  • Genomic screening by array CGH was completed for all patients, together with the analysis of the genomic profiles and the classification of CNVs according to their potential contribution to pathogenesis;
  • The experimental protocol for next-generation whole-exome sequencing was carried out on selected samples and the data obtained were analysed and interpreted;
  • Scientific papers were presented at three European conferences and two national conferences; one original article was published and one manuscript with original results was submitted for review to a journal indexed in Web of Science, and was accepted for publication during the first semester of 2022.

Stage 4 Dec 2022

Objectives of stage 4

  • Evaluation of the preliminary genetic data, as well as of the clinical and imaging data, in collaboration with partner 5 of the consortium, with a view to applying whole-exome sequencing – WES
  • Mutational screening by next-generation whole-exome sequencing of the selected patients and their parents (open-exome)
  • Analysis of the data and interpretation of the results generated, with a view to detecting new variants in known genes or candidate genes with the potential to constitute therapeutic targets
  • Dissemination of the results through presentation of papers at national and international conferences and publication of scientific articles

Methodologies used and results obtained

In the current stage, investigations based on next-generation whole-exome sequencing were carried out for five patients and their parents. The proposed group of 12 trio WES investigations (patient and both parents) was thus completed.

The biological samples consisted of peripheral blood, collected from the patients and their parents. The library preparation protocol for sequencing was carried out for 16 selected biological samples. The libraries obtained were denatured and sequenced on the NextSeq 550 platform (Illumina) with the NextSeq 500/550 300-cycle kit. The data were analysed and interpreted using the NextGene software (SoftGenetics), in successive steps. The module created in the previous phase for individual analysis (single case) and the comparative analysis module (trio analysis) were used. In the first step of the analysis, particular attention was paid to genes known to be involved in the occurrence of brain malformations. Subsequently, the other protein-coding genes were analysed. The clinical significance of the detected variants was assessed by querying population databases (for example GnomAD) and databases of variants reported in patients with neurodevelopmental pathologies (ClinVar, Decipher, OMIM).

Dissemination activities were carried out by presenting one paper at each of four international conferences (the European Human Genetics Conference, the Congress of the European Paediatric Neurology Society, the International Congress of Paediatric Neurology and the Excellence in Pediatrics Conference) and at the Congress of the Romanian Society of Medical Genetics. The original scientific article “Novel DCX pathogenic variant in a girl with subcortical band heterotopia” (DOI:10.2478/rrlm-2022-0031) was published in Revista Romana de Medicina de Laborator (impact factor 1.027). In addition, a manuscript with original results entitled „Autistic behavior as novel clinical finding in OFD1 syndrome” was submitted for review to the journal Genes, indexed in WEB of Science (impact factor 4.141), and was published in January 2023.

In conclusion, in this stage the proposed stage objectives were achieved:

  • The collection of biological samples was finalised;
  • The experimental protocol for next-generation whole-exome sequencing was carried out and libraries were obtained for 5 biological samples, thus totalling the 12 proposed trio tests, plus the analysis of two additional patients;
  • Whole-exome sequencing data from 5 patients were analysed and interpreted, thus completing the group of 14 patients tested by WES, by correlating the data with those from the literature and those obtained by different groups of specialists, including within the HETEROMICS consortium;
  • The functional impact of the detected variants was evaluated in silico, using different functional prediction programs to classify their contribution to pathogenesis;
  • Scientific papers were presented at 4 European conferences and one national conference; one original article was published and one manuscript with original results was submitted for review to a journal indexed in WEB of Science.
