Evaluation of the kinomic and miRNomic profile for the exploration of new molecular targets and combinatorial therapies in glioblastoma; omics approaches

Through its aim and stated objectives, the proposed project falls under Objective 1 of the Nucleu Programme proposal, Identification and/or validation of molecular targets in tumour and degenerative pathology, and will be carried out in the Laboratory of Biochemistry-Proteomics of INCD Victor Babeş.

The aim of the project is the evaluation of the kinomic and miRNomic profile, with a view to exploiting new molecular targets in combinatorial therapies – kinase inhibitors: synthetic and bioactive natural compounds. This study will be carried out by evaluating the impact of modulating certain signalling nodes on protein targets and on epigenetic regulators -miRNA- in glioblastoma (GBM).

Some of the most important signalling cascades deregulated in GBM are PI3K/Akt/mTOR and MAPK; their activation leads to the development of drug resistance. Owing to their post-transcriptional regulatory function, miRNAs play a central role in the signalling process.

The project proposes a systemic approach comprising the evaluation of the deregulation of signalling networks, with regard to the new concept of the relationship between key signalling molecules and miRNA. The study of miRNA deregulation in relation to signalling in glioblastoma could offer a new perspective for innovative combinatorial therapies, conventional and based on bioactive natural compounds.

Elements of novelty and originality are provided by the generation of panels of signalling molecules selected following the modulation of key nodes of the signalling networks by synthetic blockers or bioactive natural compounds. The data will generate comparative signalling maps between basal activity and experimentally induced modified activity. This systemic approach represents a novelty in the field of life sciences: the correlation between signalling proteins and selected miRNAs, the kinomic and miRNomic profile, creating the framework for their future confirmation/validation as possible targets in targeted/personalised combinatorial therapies.

Objective1

Development of conceptual models for proteomic, transcriptomic and epigenetic approaches. Design and development of experimental methods and models. Selection and development of primary cell cultures derived from tumour tissue and of standardised cell lines.

Objective2

Evaluation of the kinomic profile following the modulation of intracellular signalling:

– analysis of a panel of surface receptors involved in the pathogenesis of glioblastoma by multiplex proteomic approaches.

– analysis of signalling nodes: total and phosphorylated proteins;

– modulation of signalling by PI3K and MAPK inhibition.

Objective3

Evaluation of the miRNomic profile following the modulation of intracellular signalling:

– identification of miRNAs regulating epigenetic mechanisms (high-throughput screening for miRNA);

– evaluation of epigenetic changes at the level of genomic DNA;

– analysis of the degree of global methylation/proteins involved.

Objective4

Bioactive natural compounds – epigenetic and signalling modulators.

Objective5

Validation, establishment of correlations and development of a potential combinatorial therapy algorithm in glioblastomas.

Secondary objectives

  • Implementation of the concepts of kinomic profile/miRNomic profile as a systemic approach in evaluating the molecular signature of glioblastoma;
  • Configuration of therapeutic combinations: synthetic kinase inhibitors plus bioactive natural compounds;
  • Dissemination of the results obtained through publications and communications;
  • Training through research of the PhD students and postdoctoral researchers involved in the project;
  • Establishment of collaborations with other research institutes and specialised clinics interested in developing and applying these innovative concepts;
  • Development/establishment of international cooperation in the field of the project for the preparation of joint research projects
