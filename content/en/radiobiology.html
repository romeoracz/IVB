Radiobiology Laboratory

Main scientific mission to investigate the redox changes in Health and Disease, with special focus on radiobiology and astrobiology.

The main research theme of the Radiobiology Laboratory is redox medicine and therapy applied to radio- and photodynamic therapies in cancer, as well as the interaction of space-relevant ionising radiation with normal cells in the context of astrobiology. In the field of radiobiology, we collaborate with the innovative Extreme Light – Nuclear Physics infrastructure (ELI-NP, Romania) built by the EU in Romania and with the Heavy Ion Research Centre (GSI) in Germany to investigate the response of human cells to radiotherapy or, respectively, to the components of galactic cosmic radiation.

Through a systems biology approach, we aim to connect cellular responses to the network of molecular changes triggered by different radiation fields. Of particular interest in our research is the cytoprotective transcription factor NRF2, which controls the expression of over 250 stress, antioxidant and detoxifying genes. On the one hand, pharmacological inhibition of NRF2 could increase the efficacy of cancer therapy, while, on the other hand, its activation is expected to protect normal cells against the harmful action of ionising radiation during occupational exposure, including astronauts on their journey into space. In the field of NRF2 medicine and therapy we have an ongoing collaboration with the BenBedPhar consortium.

In addition to research in radiobiology, we are currently investigating the inflammation – redox relationship in chronic diseases such as cardiovascular and neurodegenerative diseases, with the aim of identifying the molecular signature of inflammation and redox signalling in order to characterise the associated pathological networks and to identify promising biomarkers and therapeutic targets in these pathologies.
