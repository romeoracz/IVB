Cell Biology, Neuroscience and Experimental Myology

A RESEARCH UNIT THAT COMBINES THE EXPERTISE OF A RESEARCH TEAM WITH SOLID EXPERIENCE IN CELL BIOLOGY AND NEUROSCIENCE.

Our research activity currently focuses on understanding the cellular and molecular details of physiological and pathological mechanisms across 4 distinct research themes:

  • Leukaemia: the mechanism of conversion of pre-leukaemic haematopoietic progenitors into leukaemia-initiating cells and the establishment of new mutation detection methods to monitor minimal residual disease (MRD) and chimerism after bone marrow transplantation
  • Neurodegenerative diseases: Characterisation of the effect of bacterial functional amyloid proteins and other amyloidogenic products of the gut microbiota on pathological protein aggregation and on the prion-like propagation of pathology in models of neurodegeneration, with a view to identifying biomarkers and molecular targets for the development of personalised prophylactic and therapeutic strategies. in sporadic Parkinson's disease and other neurodegenerative diseases, thus contributing to an improved approach to pathologies with a major impact on health.
  • Skeletal muscle regeneration in normal and dystrophic muscles, with the aim of identifying new and potentially more reliable biomarkers of disease progression for chronic and genetic diseases.
  • Cancer research: epigenetic changes at the level of DNA methylation and post-translational histone modifications; Natural bioactive compounds acting as epigenetic modulators in chemoprevention – in vitro studies.
