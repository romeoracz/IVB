The Medical Genetics Laboratory

is involved in the study of genetic causes and pathogenic mechanisms in two important areas of human pathology

  • The laboratory has two major research directions:

    • Identification of genomic imbalances and DNA sequence variations in neurodevelopmental disorders, with a focus on autism spectrum disorders, brain malformations, epilepsy and unexplained intellectual disabilities. The detailed clinical and genetic data, obtained through genome-wide microarray studies and classical and next-generation sequencing, contribute to defining potentially new clinical entities and to further delineating existing syndromes.
    • Discovery and molecular characterisation of genetic abnormalities in haematological malignancies. Using genomic technologies, our laboratory has focused on identifying clinically relevant oncogenic variants and co-occurrence patterns in acute myeloid leukaemia. The identification of recurrent and novel molecular lesions allows better insight into disease pathogenesis and improved detection / prediction tools.

    Our laboratory aims to define the underlying genetic defects in unique patient samples and to correlate them with clinical data, in close collaboration with clinical departments, ultimately aiming at a better understanding of the disease mechanisms in these patients.
