PN 23.16.01.02

Sarcopenia associated with neurodegenerative diseases: defining the molecular basis of the inter-relationship along the brain–striated muscle axis for the identification of prognostic factors and therapeutic targets

Contract no. 10N/2023

Funder: Ministry of Research, Innovation and Digitalisation

Duration: 2023-2025

Within the Nucleu Programme: Molecular mechanisms in major non-communicable diseases – from cancer to degenerative pathology, from the genetic to the immune dimension (MEMOGEN)

Objective: Identification of the molecular mechanisms that are determinant in the pathogenesis of non-communicable diseases, with diagnostic and therapeutic impact

Project leader: CS I Laura Ceafalan

Project activities and progress Indicators and dissemination of results Publications and scientific dissemination

Project presentation

Neurodegenerative diseases are associated, in their advanced stages, with significant changes in skeletal muscle, which may contribute to reduced mobility and quality of life of patients.

The project investigates the molecular mechanisms involved in the communication between skeletal muscle and the central nervous system, with a focus on Parkinson’s disease. Particular attention is paid to circulating molecules with a role in inter-tissue communication, such as myokines and microRNAs (miRNA), as well as to the epigenetic mechanisms that may take part in this interaction.

To investigate the brain–muscle axis, in vitro and in vivo experimental models are used, which allow the study of interactions between neuronal and muscle cells and the evaluation of the influence of physical exercise on the molecular mechanisms analysed.

Aim of the project

The aim of the project is to identify and characterise circulating muscle biomarkers altered in neurodegenerative diseases associated with muscle impairment, with a potential role in assessing disease prognosis and in identifying new therapeutic targets.

The project seeks to characterise molecular communication along the brain–muscle axis through the integrated analysis of myokines, miRNAs and certain epigenetic mechanisms, using experimental models of Parkinson’s disease.

Objectives

OB1. Establishing a panel of biomarkers for inter-tissue cooperation through soluble molecules, using in vitro experimental models based on co-cultures of dopaminergic neurons and muscle cells.

OB2. Evaluating the effect of moderate physical training on the onset and progression of muscle impairment in the animal model of Parkinson’s disease.

OB3. Establishing a panel of circulating biomarkers in the in vivo experimental model, for investigating inter-tissue cooperation through soluble molecules and identifying potential prognostic factors for the progression of Parkinson’s disease and the onset of muscle impairment.

OB4. Establishing a panel of circulating biomarkers with prognostic potential in patients with Parkinson’s disease with different degrees of muscle impairment.

Project activities and progress

2023 – Phase 1

Evaluation of inter-tissue cooperation through soluble molecules in in vitro experimental models

In the first stage of the project, the in vitro experimental models needed to investigate the communication between muscle cells and dopaminergic neurons were developed and optimised.

The activities included the optimisation of the culture and differentiation conditions for human muscle cells and the validation of the differentiation process by morphological and molecular methods.

A co-culture system between differentiated muscle cells and dopaminergic neurons was developed and characterised, under experimental conditions that allow the comparison of cellular interactions in the presence or absence of changes associated with the Parkinson’s disease phenotype.

Within these experimental systems, the profiles of certain myokines involved in intercellular communication were analysed, both at cellular level and in the culture medium. The analyses were performed using complementary techniques, including protein multiplexing and Western blot.

In parallel, miRNA expression profiles were investigated and candidates were selected for further analyses.

Methods for the evaluation of certain epigenetic markers were also optimised, including the analysis of the degree of genomic DNA methylation.

The activities of this phase made it possible to establish the in vitro experimental platform needed to investigate the molecular dialogue between muscle and nervous tissue.

2024 – Phase 2

Obtaining and characterising the in vivo experimental model of Parkinson’s disease

In the second stage, the research was extended from cellular systems to in vivo experimental models.

Animal models of Parkinson’s disease were evaluated and the experimental groups needed to study the muscular and neurological changes associated with the disease were established.

An important component of this phase was the evaluation of the effect of moderate physical exercise. Wheel training protocols for rodents were developed and tested, in order to investigate the influence of physical activity on muscle tissue and on the molecular parameters monitored within the project.

Samples of skeletal muscle, brain and blood were collected and processed for histological, molecular and biochemical analyses.

Muscle tissue was characterised by histological, histochemical and morphometric methods, with attention to tissue organisation, the characteristics of the different types of muscle fibres and parameters associated with mitochondrial function.

In parallel, the methods needed to investigate a panel of myokines with a role in muscle function and metabolism, the response to exercise and communication with the central nervous system were optimised.

This stage made it possible to consolidate the in vivo experimental model and to build the groups and the collection of biological samples needed for the next stage of the project.

2025 – Phase 3

Investigation of the biomarkers involved in communication along the brain–muscle axis

In this stage, research focused on characterising the biomarkers involved in communication between skeletal muscle and the central nervous system in the experimental model of Parkinson’s disease.

The experimental groups were completed and animals were analysed according to several biological and experimental parameters, including genotype, age, sex and the performance of physical training.

Samples of skeletal muscle, brain and plasma were collected from the animals included in the study, which allowed the comparative investigation of the same molecular mechanisms in different biological compartments.

Myokine analysis

A panel of myokines with a role in muscle homeostasis, metabolism, inflammation, regeneration and the response to physical exercise was investigated.

Their expression was evaluated in muscle and brain tissue samples and in circulating biological samples, in order to characterise the relationship between the peripheral compartment and the central nervous system.

Groups with and without changes associated with Parkinson’s disease and groups with and without physical exercise were compared, in order to evaluate the influence of these factors on the molecular profiles investigated.

miRNA analysis

In parallel with the myokine analysis, miRNAs selected on the basis of their relevance to the mechanisms studied within the project were investigated.

Methods for the analysis of miRNAs in tissue and circulating samples were optimised and applied, with a view to integrating these data with the myokine expression profiles.

Analysis of epigenetic markers

Investigations into the epigenetic mechanisms associated with muscular and neurological changes were continued.

The analyses included the evaluation of the degree of genomic DNA methylation and the investigation of certain molecular mechanisms involved in epigenetic regulation and the cellular stress response.

2026 – Phase 4

Clinical validation of the biomarkers involved in communication along the muscle–brain axis in Parkinson’s disease

In the current stage, research will be extended towards the clinical validation of the biomarkers identified and selected in the previous experimental stages of the project.

The study will evaluate a targeted panel of circulating biomarkers in patients with Parkinson’s disease and in control subjects, in order to investigate their association with disease severity and with impaired muscle function.

Groups of patients at different stages of Parkinson’s disease will be analysed comparatively. The molecular data will be correlated with clinical parameters relevant to disease severity, motor function and muscle function.

One component of the study will seek to integrate the profile of circulating myokines with the analysis of selected miRNAs, in order to evaluate a molecular signature associated with the muscle–brain axis.

This stage seeks to translate the results obtained in the in vitro and in vivo experimental models to human pathology and to evaluate the potential of the biomarkers studied for patient stratification and for the further development of tools with prognostic value.

Status: clinical validation stage in progress.

Data integration

The molecular data obtained from plasma, muscle and brain are analysed comparatively to characterise communication along the brain–muscle axis and to identify candidate biomarkers relevant to disease progression and the associated muscle impairment.

The integrated analysis also seeks to evaluate the influence of physical exercise on the molecular mechanisms studied.

Methods used

To achieve the project objectives, complementary methods of cell and molecular biology, histology and protein analysis are used, including:

  • cell cultures and neuron–muscle co-culture systems;
  • immunofluorescence and microscopy;
  • Western blot;
  • Luminex xMAP multiplexing;
  • RT-PCR and qRT-PCR;
  • ddPCR;
  • miRNA analysis;
  • DNA methylation analysis;
  • muscle histology and histochemistry;
  • morphometric analysis;
  • statistical analysis and data integration methods.

Relevance of the project

The integrated study of skeletal muscle, the central nervous system and circulating biomarkers contributes to a better understanding of the mechanisms involved in the interaction between muscle impairment and neurodegeneration.

The approach used in the project allows the brain–muscle axis to be investigated from the perspective of inter-tissue molecular communication, and the evaluation of how this may be influenced by disease progression and physical activity.

The results obtained within the project are being analysed, exploited and disseminated through publications and specialist scientific events.

Expected results of the project

  • proof-of-concept in vitro and in vivo experimental models for studying communication along the brain–muscle axis;
  • characterisation of molecules involved in communication between muscle tissue and the central nervous system;
  • identification of myokine and miRNA profiles with potential relevance to the progression of neurodegenerative diseases;
  • characterisation of epigenetic mechanisms associated with the processes investigated;
  • selection of candidate biomarkers for further validation studies.

Indicators and dissemination of results

The activities carried out within the project led to the development and use of in vitro and in vivo experimental models, the optimisation of molecular and tissue analysis methods and the dissemination of results through publications and participation in scientific events.
