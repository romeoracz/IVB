PN 23.16.01.03

THE FUNCTIONAL TRIAD IN EXPERIMENTAL MURINE PSORIASIS: IMMUNE SYSTEM – SKIN - GUT

Duration: 2023 – 2026

Project director: CSI Dr Carolina Constantin, Immunology Laboratory

Project objective

The general objective of this research project is to characterise the functional mechanisms and the inter-system communication gut – skin – immune system in autoimmune diseases (experimental murine psoriasis), with translation of the therapy validated in this preclinical study into human cases. The aim of the project is to identify the pathogenic changes in the gut – skin – immune system functional triad in experimental murine psoriasis, before and after adjuvant therapies. Thus, the gut-skin-immune system triad defines a new concept with potential applicability in elucidating the molecular mechanisms involved in the pathogenesis of psoriasis and with an impact on (personalised) therapeutic modulation.

Expected results for achieving the objective

To achieve the general objective of the project, the following will be considered:

  • Assessment of the cellular immune status in acute and chronic experimental murine psoriasis (Ps).
  • Proteomic investigation of the inflammatory syndrome in experimental Ps (acute and chronic).
  • Immunological and proteomic modulation of the inflammatory syndrome in experimental Ps using unconventional therapies – treatment with the specific IgY protein component.
  • Immunological and proteomic modulation of the inflammatory syndrome in experimental Ps using unconventional therapies – hypocaloric diet.
  • Assessment and characterisation of the dynamics of the murine microbiome (skin/gut) in the development of experimental Ps.
  • Modulation of the microbiome (skin / gut) in murine Ps using unconventional therapies.

This research project aims to achieve the following targets at the end of the implementation period:

  • characterisation of the inflammatory syndrome through a complex clinical / histopathological / immunological / proteomic approach to the experimental model of acute and chronic/transgenic Ps, before and after adjuvant therapies;
  • characterisation of the immune system – skin – gut functional triad in experimental Ps before and after adjuvant therapies, with an impact on diagnosis and therapy.

STAGE 2023

Phase title: Assessment of the cellular and humoral immune status in acute experimental murine Ps

Phase objective: To achieve this objective, the experimental murine model of acute Ps induced by application of Imiquimod (IMQ)-based cream was established and subsequently characterised clinically, histopathologically and immunologically. For this experimental model, the main immune cell populations and subpopulations in peripheral blood and spleen were investigated by flow cytometry in order to assess the cellular immune status. To assess the humoral immune status in acute experimental murine Ps, a broad panel of inflammatory cytokines possibly involved in the pathogenesis of Ps was tested. Also, the experimental murine model of acute Ps was characterised proteomically, by measuring circulating cytokines/chemokines.

Experimental design for characterising the cellular and humoral immune status in Ps

Stage results

  1. The experimental murine model of acute Ps induced by application of IMQ-based cream to the dorsal region was established and evaluated clinically, histopathologically, immunologically and proteomically.
  2. Disease progression and the severity of the induced inflammation were assessed on the basis of in vivo measurements (for erythema, induration and desquamation), the PASI score, splenomegaly and histopathological evaluation. The results obtained revealed that the acute Ps experimental model established showed characteristics specific to human Ps, being reproducible and functional.
  3. To assess the cellular immune status in acute experimental Ps, the lymphocyte populations and subpopulations in peripheral blood and splenocyte suspensions were identified and quantified, revealing statistically significant changes for all parameters investigated.
  4. Analysis of the cellular parameters in peripheral blood revealed a statistically significant decrease in the mean percentage values of T-CD4+ and B lymphocytes in mice from the Ps group vs the control group, and a statistically significant increase in the mean percentage values of T-CD8a+ lymphocytes and NK cells. For the T-CD4+/T-CD8a+ ratio, statistically significant decreases in values were recorded in mice from the Ps group compared with the control group, as a consequence of the distribution of T lymphocyte subsets in peripheral blood.
  5. The distribution of cellular parameters in the spleen revealed a similar trend of variation, except for NK cells, where a decrease in values was recorded in mice with Ps, and the differences obtained between the two experimental groups were statistically significant.
  6. To assess the humoral status in acute experimental murine Ps, a broad panel of inflammatory cytokines possibly involved in the pathogenesis of Ps was tested, in order to assess changes in serum levels in IMQ-induced experimental Ps; statistically significant changes in circulating levels were obtained for IL-1α, IL-1β, IL-5, IL-6, IL-9, IL-10, IL-12 (p70), IL-13, IL-15, IL-17, IFN-γ, TNF-α, IP-10/CXCL10, MCP-1/CCL2, MIP-1α/CCL3, MIP-1β/CCL4, MIG/CXCL9 and KC /CXCL1.
  7. For pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g. TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-1β, IL-15, IL-17) and a number of chemokines (e.g. MCP-1/CCL2, MIP-1α/CCL3, MIP-1β/CCL4, KC/CXCL1, IP-10/CXCL10, MIG/CXCL9), significantly increased serum levels were obtained in Ps mice compared with normal mice. A serum profile of inflammatory cytokines and chemokines involved in IMQ-induced acute experimental murine Ps was established.

STAGE 2024

Phase title: Assessment of the cellular and humoral immune status in chronic experimental murine Ps

Phase objective: assessment of the cellular immune status in chronic experimental murine Ps. To achieve this objective, an attempt was made to establish an experimental murine model of chronic/systemic Ps induced by application of Imiquimod (IMQ)-based cream concomitantly with intraperitoneal injection of IMQ. This model was characterised clinically, histopathologically and immunologically. The immunological characterisation was based on quantification by flow cytometry of the main immune cell populations and subpopulations in peripheral blood and spleen, thus assessing the cellular immune status in murine chronic/systemic Ps. Also, for this experimental murine model, circulating inflammatory cytokines/chemokines were measured.

Stage results

  1. The experimental murine model of chronic Ps with a systemic component was established and evaluated clinically, histopathologically and immunologically. Disease progression and the severity of inflammation were assessed on the basis of weekly in vivo measurements for erythema, induration and desquamation, the PASI score, splenomegaly, hepatomegaly and histopathological evaluation. The results obtained revealed that the chronic Ps experimental model with a systemic component established showed characteristics specific to human Ps, being reproducible and functional.
  2. Analysis of the cellular parameters in peripheral blood revealed a statistically significant decrease in the mean % values of B lymphocytes in Ps mice vs the control group. A decrease in the mean % values of T-CD8a+ lymphocytes and NK cells was observed, but without statistical relevance.
  3. The distribution of cellular parameters in the spleen revealed a statistically significant decrease in the mean percentage values of B lymphocytes and NK cells in the chronic Ps group compared with the control group. Also, a reversal of the trend of variation observed in peripheral blood was recorded for T-CD8a+ lymphocytes, for which an increase in mean percentage values was recorded in mice from the chronic Ps group, but without statistical significance.
  4. Using protein microarray technology, a broad panel of inflammatory cytokines possibly involved in the pathogenesis of Ps was tested, in order to assess changes in serum levels in IMQ-induced experimental Ps; statistically significant changes in circulating levels were obtained both for cytokines from the Th1 panel (e.g., IL-1a, IL-6, IL-15) and for cytokines from the Th2 panel (e.g., IL-5, IL-10, IL-13). In addition, the data on the chemokine profile in the chronic inflammatory process of the skin (e.g., CCL11, MIP-1 gamma) also focus the direction of the study on the chemokine subcategory, underlining the need to reposition their role in other pathologies as well – Ps – than those where studies are more numerous (atopic dermatitis, allergies). Thus, the serum profile of inflammatory cytokines and chemokines involved in IMQ-induced chronic experimental murine Ps was established.

STAGE 2025 (ongoing)

Phase title: Immunological and proteomic modulation of the inflammatory syndrome in experimental psoriasis using unconventional therapies

Phase objective: assessment of the cellular and humoral immune status in experimental murine Ps treated with IgY isolated from hyperimmune egg. To achieve this objective, the experimental murine model of Ps induced by application of IMQ-based cream was established, and this model was subjected to an unconventional therapy – the IgY protein component isolated from the yolk of hyperimmune eggs, from hens immunised with antibiotic-resistant pathogenic bacteria. The experimental model was characterised clinically, histopathologically and immuno-proteomically. The main immune cell populations and subpopulations (in peripheral blood and spleen) were investigated and circulating inflammatory cytokines/chemokines were quantified, in order to assess the cellular and humoral immune status.

Experimental scheme for the further course of the project

https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4426/13/11/1556

Partial stage results

  1. The IMQ-induced experimental murine model of Ps was established and evaluated clinically, histopathologically and immunoproteomically, before and after treatment with the IgY protein component.
  2. To assess the cellular immune status in experimental Ps before IgY therapy, the lymphocyte populations/subpopulations in peripheral blood and splenocyte suspensions were identified and quantified, with statistically significant changes for all parameters investigated.
  3. In peripheral blood, a statistically significant decrease in the mean % values of T-CD4+ and B lymphocytes was observed in mice with induced Ps vs the Ctrl group, and a statistically significant increase in the mean % values of T-CD8a+ lymphocytes and NK cells.
  4. Analysis of the splenocyte suspension revealed that in mice with induced Ps, statistically significant decreases in the mean % values of T-CD4+ and B lymphocytes and NK cells were recorded, together with significant increases in T-CD8a+ lymphocyte values.
  5. To assess the humoral immune status in experimental Ps before IgY therapy, a number of inflammatory cytokines/chemokines with a possible role in the pathogenesis of Ps were quantified.
  6. Analysis of serum cytokines revealed significantly increased serum levels of IL-6, IL-1β, IFN-γ, TNF-α, IL-5, IL-10, IL-17, MCP-1 (CCL2), MIP-1α (CCL3), RANTES (CCL5), PF4 (CXCL4), ICAM-1, TIMP-1, leptin and eotaxin in mice with induced Ps compared with healthy mice.
  7. For the IMQ-induced experimental murine model of Ps thus established and characterised, the effect of treatment with the IgY protein component on the course of the clinical condition was followed, as well as the dynamics of the cellular and humoral immune status.
  8. Following the unconventional therapy – oral treatment with IgY – an improvement in the clinical course was observed, as well as a normalisation of the cellular and humoral parameters investigated. As IgY can be prepared in a specific and individualised manner, this product could open a personalised field in the adjuvant therapy of Ps.
