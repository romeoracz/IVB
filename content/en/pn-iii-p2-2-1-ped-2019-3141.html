Abstract

The project starts from an existing technological concept (TRL2) – a platform for the capture of circulating stem cells – which it will advance to TRL3. Using recent knowledge from fundamental research, the project aims to create a nanostructured substrate, dual-functionalised with anti-CD36 and anti-EpCAM (Epithelial cell adhesion molecule), in order to increase the capture rate of metastatic tumour cells from blood. The project brings two improvements to existing systems: 1- increased cell adhesion through nanostructuring of the surface and 2- increased efficiency of identification of metastatic cells by adding anti-CD36 functionalisation. The novelty of the project lies in increasing the sensitivity/specificity of the platform by functionalising the substrate for a receptor (CD36) whose expression is more consistently present on metastatic tumour cells than the classic EpCAM marker used in the existing technological concept. The CO's expertise in cellular and molecular biology, combined with P1's expertise in nanostructured, functionalised substrates and their integration into microfluidic systems, will generate a product that will demonstrate the project concept (proof of concept) at experimental level (TRL3). The expertise of the SME involved in the project in the validation of cellular diagnostic techniques will be used to prepare the technology for future validation at TRL4.

Summary of Stages 1-2

Substrates based on nanocrystalline graphite and vertical graphene were selected, because they did not bind
normal and tumour cells in suspension with high affinity, and at low cell concentrations (2000
cells/mL), the binding percentage was below 5%. This lack of non-specific
cell–substrate interaction is a guarantee of the specificity provided by subsequent functionalisation.
Optimal functionalisation depended on the type of substrate structuring: for nanocrystalline
graphite, cysteamine gave optimal results, and for vertical graphene – protein A. For
testing cell adhesion, a standardised line of normal human monocytes and a
standardised breast cancer cell line were used. To test the impact of the CD36 receptor
on tumour cell capture and on the efficiency of the functionalised substrate, which is the aim of the next
stage, these lines were genetically edited so that they no longer express this protein.

Summary of Stage 3

In this stage, the CO tested functionalised and non-functionalised substrates (supplied by P1) with several cell types with different expression profiles of CD36 and EpCAM, to see their usefulness for capturing various cell types (MCF-7 and CRL 9855), starting from the idea that the cellular phenotype differs between a cell in situ and a metastasising cell in the blood or a cell from a metastatic site. Finally, to come closer to the biological relevance of the project, we used human blood samples from healthy volunteers, to which we added a very small number of tumour cells, which we incubated on the selected substrates in order to test capture efficiency. With the help of the private-sector partners (P2), we produced a prototype model for the rapid determination of the capture of tumour cells from blood.
