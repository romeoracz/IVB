Automated screening platform for small molecules with anti-inflammatory potential

https://marichiritoiu4.wixsite.com/ped337

Scientific context: The immune system is the main pillar of human health. An in-depth understanding of immune modulators will make it possible to decipher the mechanisms of the immune system in the fight against a wide variety of human pathologies. Chronic inflammation is the main feature of many pathologies, including cancer, and anti-inflammatory therapy currently focuses on reducing the production of eicosanoid mediators of inflammation (prostaglandins, thromboxanes, prostacyclins, leukotrienes) by inhibiting endogenous cyclooxygenases (COX-1/2). Although effective, recent studies have highlighted potential side effects of these molecules. Consequently, the identification and validation of new molecules with anti-inflammatory potential and reduced side effects is of real interest. Recently, the development of molecules that target the signalling pathways involved in the production and release of pro-inflammatory mediators, such as cytokines and chemokines, has gained ground and promotes the concept of new-generation therapies.

Hypothesis: The identification of molecules that target the production and secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines by macrophages and monocytes, such as interleukin-1β (IL-1β), will enable an improved alternative treatment for the chronic inflammation associated with numerous pathologies.

Aim: This project aims to develop an automated screening platform by generating a reporter cell line with endogenously tagged IL-1β, using CRISPR-CAS9 technology, to monitor the secretion of this cytokine, with the aim of identifying new compounds with anti-inflammatory activity that will be validated in primary macrophages and animal models of sepsis.

Expected results: The implementation of this project will generate a versatile screening platform for anti-inflammatory compounds and a list of validated molecules with anti-inflammatory properties, which can be easily translated into preclinical studies.
