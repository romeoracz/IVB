Abstract

The main objective of the project is the preclinical development of a personalised photodynamic therapy (PDT) protocol using innovative porphyrin photosensitisers and redox modulators, smartly formulated for the treatment of premalignant skin lesions, such as actinic keratosis (AK). The project falls within the Bioeconomy – Biotechnology theme.

The medical application will be demonstrated using cells and mouse models. Both the therapeutic efficacy and the toxicological aspects of the new PDT protocol will be investigated. We will start the project at TLR 2 – formulation of the technology/product concept, relying on a portfolio of six patents and the team’s expertise in PDT and redox medicine. We will advance through TRL 3 – validation of the experimental concept, through in vitro and ex vivo studies using cell lines and mouse skin explants. We expect to reach TLR 4 at the end of the project – laboratory validation of the technology/method, by testing in an animal model an innovative PDT keratolysis protocol, with application in AK, based on innovative photosensitisers and redox modulators.

6) A workshop organised at the end of the project to present the results to stakeholders (academia, dermatologists and pharmaceutical SMEs).

7) New research services for the preclinical testing of new PDT strategies in dermatology, to be implemented at: IVB (PDT on tumour cells and small laboratory animals) and at BTH (in vitro PDT on skin-relevant cells, technological process for the formulation of porphyrins and photochemical redox modulators), to be published in eERIS.

9) Dedicated web page and media channels to promote the knowledge generated in the project to specialists, the pharmaceutical industry and the general public.

SUMMARY OF STAGE I/2022

Synthesis and preliminary characterisation of the new porphyrin compounds

Recent statistics highlight cancer as the leading cause of death worldwide, forecasting an increase in the number of cases of 47% by 2040 compared with 2020 [1,2]. For these reasons, the development of innovative strategies for disseminating prevention measures and providing active substances with antitumour potential is essential for combating cancer worldwide. Non-melanoma lesions (actinic keratoses, squamous cell and basal cell carcinoma, cutaneous lymphoma, Kaposi sarcoma, angiosarcoma) are among the most frequent forms of cancer. The development of a non-melanoma cancer is determined by genetic factors, history of HPV infection, history of chronic inflammatory skin conditions, exposure to toxic substances, to the sun, immunosuppression, skin phototypes I and II, PUVA as previous therapy [3, 4]. Actinic keratoses are described in the literature as “carcinoma in situ” (because of the dysplastic keratinocytes resembling squamous cell carcinoma) or as a premalignant lesion [5]. The therapeutic approach to actinic keratosis must aim to reduce the risk of malignant transformation and at early diagnosis of malignant formations that may appear in a field of actinic keratosis. Porphyrins, through their structural and spectral profile, have potential in the identification and therapy of malignant skin manifestations [6]. In addition, the major advantage of these haem-like structural types is their selectivity for tumour cells and their versatility, the possibility of structural modelling by attaching substituents with different degrees of polarity, to obtain an optimal hydrophilic/lipophilic ratio for cellular internalisation.

In this context, the main objective of the project is to develop a protocol for the photodynamic therapy (PDT) of actinic keratosis, using new porphyrin derivatives. The project activities include the evaluation of the potential as a marker and antitumour agent of porphyrin compounds with asymmetric molecular architecture and an appropriate spectral profile. A first stage of the project provides for obtaining new porphyrin structures, through ecological synthesis techniques that comply with current requirements in the field of drug synthesis. The aim was to obtain a set of data associated with the synthesis procedures and the parameters defining the structural profile of the new photosensitisers.

In Activity I.1, carried out by UMF, the following porphyrin compounds were obtained:

  • with asymmetric structures:
  • 5-(2,4-dihydroxyphenyl)-10,15,20-tris-(4-acetoxy-3-methoxyphenyl) porphyrin (2)
  • 5-(2-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)-10,15,20-tris-(4-carboxymethylphenyl) porphyrin (P 5.2)
  • with symmetric structures (reference for the asymmetric derivatives in the in vitro studies)
  • 5,10,15,20-meso-tetrakis-(4-acetoxy-3-methoxyphenyl) porphyrin (P4.1º1)
  • 5, 10,15,20-tris-(4-carboxymethylphenyl) porphyrin (P 5.1)

The structural and spectral evaluation of the synthesised compounds was carried out in Activity I.1 by NMR, FTIR, UV-Vis and fluorescence analysis. Correlation of the data obtained confirmed the structures of the porphyrin compounds synthesised in this stage. In conclusion, the preliminary studies led to obtaining, with good yields, 4 porphyrin compounds by adopting a modern and ecological synthesis method.

In Activity I.2, 3 types of formulations for topical applications of the porphyrin compounds and modulators were analysed, with the gel method being selected and detailed in a first stage.

The in vitro study carried out in Activity I.3 showed that all the porphyrin compounds analysed, evaluated in vitro on normal and tumour skin-specific cells (HaCaT normal human keratinocytes, HS27 human dermal fibroblasts and B16F10 mouse melanoma cells) had good biocompatibility with normal and tumour skin cells. However, the following aspects are noted: 1) compound P5.2 can inhibit keratinocyte multiplication, which has therapeutic relevance in actinic keratosis, characterised by hyper-proliferation of these cells, if temporary photosensitisation of the skin is avoided; b) compounds P2.1 and P5.2, but not the porphyrin derivative P4.2, increase LDH release by dermal fibroblasts, possibly due to undesirable effects that disturb the membrane integrity of dermal fibroblasts.

All the porphyrin compounds analysed were incorporated into the normal and tumour skin cells investigated, admittedly with different intensities, thus proving fluorescent marker properties. Porphyrin compounds P2.1 and P4.2 can be considered candidates for fluorescence imaging in dermatology, and for PDT in oncological dermatology.

For the visibility of the project and its achievements, in Activity I.4 the project web page was created, which can be found at

https://www.ivb.ro/porphyderm

In Activity I.5, coordinated by UMF, the invention patent application entitled “Compus porfirinic cu potential de marcator fluorescent in dermato-oncologie”, was drawn up, with the participation of all partners. The technical documentation of the patent application claims elements of novelty relating to the obtaining and the spectral and biological evaluation of the compound 5-(2-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)-10,15,20-tris-(4-carboxymethylphenyl) porphyrin (P5.2). The invention patent application was registered with OSIM.
